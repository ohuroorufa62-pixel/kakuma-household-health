Abstract

Access to timely preventive healthcare remains a major challenge in underserved communities across Turkana County. This final project presents Hafisuhu (derived from the concept of protection and preservation of health), an integrated community-based healthcare model designed to bridge the gap between households and local health facilities. The project combines maternal and child health education, water and sanitation (WASH) promotion, nutrition screening, malaria prevention, and community referral tracking. Through a structured pilot study involving local households and community health promoters, the project demonstrated improved health awareness, earlier clinic attendance, and stronger household hygiene practices.

Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Background of the Study

Preventable diseases, maternal complications, malnutrition, and delayed medical care continue to affect vulnerable populations in arid and semi-arid regions such as Turkana County. Many health challenges originate at the household level due to limited health literacy, unsafe water practices, and distance to health facilities. The Hafisuhu project was created to protect community health through early prevention, education, and structured clinic referrals.

1.2 Problem Statement

Despite existing health facilities, many residents delay seeking medical attention until minor illnesses become severe emergencies. Fragmented health campaigns often address only one illness at a time, leaving families exposed to overlapping risks such as waterborne infections, malaria, and poor maternal nutrition. There is a clear need for a unified, community-centered approach that addresses core health needs together.

1.3 Project Objectives

General Objective: To design and evaluate an integrated community healthcare model (Hafisuhu) that improves preventive health practices and clinic referrals at the household level.

Specific Objectives:

To assess baseline health knowledge and hygiene practices among target households.

To implement multi-topic health education covering maternal care, WASH, nutrition, malaria, and mental well-being.

To establish a simple referral tracking mechanism linking community health promoters with local clinics.

To evaluate the impact of the intervention on household health behaviors after implementation.

Chapter 2: Literature Review

Community-based healthcare models rely on trained local residents—commonly known in Kenya as Community Health Promoters (CHPs)—to deliver preventive education and basic screening. Studies in primary healthcare show that integrated interventions combining sanitation, maternal education, and nutrition screening reduce child mortality and clinic congestion more effectively than single-disease programs. Hafisuhu builds on Kenya’s Community Health Strategy by equipping promoters with unified educational guides and referral tracking tools.

3.3 Implementation Process

Step 1 (Baseline Survey): Administered structured questionnaires to measure existing knowledge on hygiene, malaria, nutrition, and clinic visits.

Step 2 (Training & Outreach): Trained local volunteers using the Hafisuhu Integrated Health Guide and conducted weekly household and group sessions.

Step 3 (Screening & Referrals): Used Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) tapes for child nutrition checks and issued duplicate referral slips for unwell individuals.

Step 4 (Endline Evaluation): Re-assessed the same households to measure behavioral changes and verified completed clinic visits.

4.2 Discussion

The results show that delivering integrated health messages in local languages significantly improves household adoption of preventive habits. Combining nutrition screening with hygiene education allowed promoters to address multiple family risks during a single home visit, saving time and building trust with caregivers.

Chapter 5: Conclusion and Recommendations

5.1 Conclusion

The Hafisuhu Integrated Community Healthcare Project successfully demonstrated that a structured, preventive, and multi-topic health approach strengthens household resilience and encourages early medical care. Empowering community health promoters with clear tools bridges the gap between homes and local clinics.

5.2 Recommendations

For Local Health Facilities: Adopt standardized duplicate referral slips to track community-to-clinic patient follow-up.

For Community Leaders: Support monthly neighborhood health circles to sustain hygiene and nutrition awareness.

For Future Projects: Digitize the Hafisuhu household register using a simple mobile app to improve data collection and long-term tracking.
